The problem

Documentation burden is consuming the ED

Clinicians need time with patients—not keyboards. Administrative load directly impacts throughput, experience, and economics.

Roughly one-third of emergency physician time is spent on documentation. That delays patient intake and discharge, underutilizes ER capacity, and contributes to hospital revenue loss at scale. Large U.S. hospitals have been estimated to face on the order of tens of millions of dollars in annual losses tied to these dynamics (per SwiftyCare market materials).

The solution

An AI platform built for the emergency department

SwiftyCare connects self-service intake, clinical data, and physician workflows so documentation keeps pace with patient flow.

SwiftyCare is a software platform that combines a patient-facing questionnaire, vital sign capture, and a clinician workspace. Large language models draft clinical text—summaries, differential-style reasoning with suggested investigations, and structured discharge documentation—while the licensed clinician reviews, edits, and finalizes every record.

The product is bilingual (English and Hebrew) and is implemented as a modern web application with a dedicated API and database—ready to evolve toward deeper hospital integration.

At a glance, the platform is designed to:

  • Self-reliant patient history entering — structured intake before or at the bedside.
  • Integrate patient data — vitals, questionnaire, and case state in one clinical view.
  • Automate physician documentation — AI-assisted drafts that clinicians control.
  • Optimize intake-to-discharge workflows — keep the case open through investigations; close when discharge is finalized.

Clinical journey

How the workflow fits together

End-to-end, a typical path through SwiftyCare looks like this—from first touch to a finalized discharge record.

  1. Patient entry The patient opens a link or scans a code, enters identifying information, and a case is created in the system.
  2. Structured intake A guided questionnaire captures demographics, medical history, medications, presenting complaints, and follow-up questions that adapt to reported symptoms.
  3. Triage vitals Staff can record blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, temperature, respiratory rate, and pain score on the case.
  4. Clinician review On the doctor dashboard, the team opens the case, generates or refreshes an AI-assisted narrative summary and differential-style analysis, and selects diagnostic investigations from recommendations (investigations only—not disease names as “tests”).
  5. Orders on the record Selected tests are stored on the case. The case remains open while results are awaited; ordering does not close the encounter.
  6. Discharge record The clinician generates a structured discharge document (multiple clinical sections), edits it, and when satisfied, finalizes it. Finalization is the step that aligns with closing the case in this MVP.

Architecture

Technology (high level)

A clean separation between experience, API, data, and model providers—so your security and procurement teams know where each concern lives.

The system is a React single-page application talking to an Express API, with MongoDB for persistence and OpenAI models for text generation. This separation keeps clinical data and AI calls on the server side under your control, subject to your hosting and contractual choices.

Before use with real patients or PHI, your organization should assess applicable regulations (for example, medical device rules, privacy laws such as GDPR or HIPAA, and local health-data requirements) and obtain appropriate approvals, agreements, and DPIAs.
